Protecting your table surface

The play mat takes the wear, cup holders keep drinks off the surface entirely, and lifting rather than dragging avoids most of the rest.

Most surface wear on a gaming table comes from four things: dice, drinks, dragging, and sunlight. All four are avoidable.

Let the mat take the wear

The neoprene Tristar Grid play mat sits in the vault and is the single most effective thing you can do. Dice land on it, miniatures slide on it, and it absorbs the abrasion the vault floor would otherwise take.

It's also the cheapest part to replace. That's the point — a worn mat is a consumable; a worn vault floor is a table.

Keep drinks off the surface entirely

Cup holders clip into the Omnitrack rail, which puts the glass beside the table rather than on it. Between a knocked drink and a ring mark, that's the whole problem solved rather than mitigated.

If you're using the table for meals with the toppers fitted, coasters and trivets still apply — see Using your table for dining.

Lift, don't drag

Drag a heavy box across a finish and it will mark, on any table at any price. Lifting game boxes, laptops, and boards on and off matters more than any product you can buy.

Same for the toppers: lift them into place rather than sliding them across the rails.

Keep it out of direct sun

Fading is one of the things every furniture warranty excludes, including ours — and it's cumulative, invisible day to day, and irreversible. A table by a south-facing window will change colour over a year or two, and unevenly.

If the room can't avoid it, a blind at the worst hours does more than anything applied to the wood.

What counts as wear rather than a defect

Scratches, dents, and fading from use are wear. A finish flaw that arrived that way is a defect. The line is whether it was there when you received the table — see Something is defective or not fitting right if you're unsure which you're looking at, and send a photo; that settles it faster than a description.
