Caring for your Zola finish

Soft dry or barely damp cloth, mild dish soap and water when it needs more, nothing stronger — and the visible grain and lighter lines in Cerused Oak are intentional, not a defect.

Two things: how to clean it, and one thing about Cerused Oak that catches people off guard.

The Cerused Oak look is intentional

The Cerused Oak finish is meant to have visible grain with lighter lines worked into the oak. That texture is part of the look — it's what ceruse is — rather than a defect, a manufacturing inconsistency, or weathering. If you were expecting a uniform surface, the first impression can read as "this looks worn," and it isn't.

Grain pattern also varies naturally piece to piece, which is a feature of real wood finishes generally.

If a finish genuinely looks wrong to you after that — not textured, but wrong — send us a photo through our contact page and we'll take a look.

Day-to-day care

  • Wipe with a soft, dry or barely damp cloth
  • When water alone won't shift something, use mild dish soap and water
  • Avoid anything stronger — no solvents, no abrasives, no strong household sprays
  • Avoid standing liquid. Wipe spills rather than letting them sit
  • Use the cup holders for drinks. That's largely what they're for, and it's the single easiest way to keep the play surface pristine

Where the table can live: indoors, climate-controlled

This one matters more than any cleaning habit. Zola tables belong in a normal heated and cooled indoor room — nowhere else.

The tables are built from solid oak, oak veneer, and premium MDF, and all three move with humidity. A garage, an unheated basement, a sunroom, or a covered patio swings too far in temperature and moisture across a year. What that looks like after a season or two is veneer lifting at an edge, panels that stop sitting flush, and finish damage — and because it comes from the environment rather than the manufacturing, none of it is covered by the warranty.

Two things to watch even indoors:

  • Direct sunlight fades a finish unevenly, and the part under a play mat or a topper fades at a different rate than the part that's exposed.
  • Direct heat — a radiator, a wood stove, or a vent blowing straight onto the table — dries wood out locally and is the fastest way to open a seam.

If your only available room is marginal, ask us before you order rather than after. Message us through our contact page and describe the space.

If you scratch or ding it

A color-matched furniture touch-up marker or wax stick is the right tool, and any furniture or hardware store carries them. Two rules make the difference between an invisible repair and a worse mark:

  1. Match in person. Take a photo of your table into the store — Dark Walnut and Cerused Oak are both harder to match from memory than you'd expect.
  2. Test somewhere hidden first. An underside, or an inside edge. Never test on the spot you're trying to fix.

Don't sand it. The visible surfaces are oak veneer, and the veneer layer is thin — sanding through it turns a scratch into something genuinely difficult to recover from. This is a fill-and-blend job, not a refinishing job. We don't sell a branded touch-up kit, and we'd rather point you at the right generic product than pretend otherwise.

Minor scratches and dents are cosmetic wear, not defects, so they sit outside warranty coverage — see Wear versus a defect. If a mark looks like something other than ordinary damage, send a photo through our contact page and we'll take a look.

Protecting the surface long-term

  • The Table Topper Set protects the recessed vault when the table is doing dining or work duty — see What the Table Topper Set does
  • The Table Topper Storage Cube ($129.99) keeps the topper panels in padded inserts when they're off the table, rather than leaning against a wall collecting scuffs
  • A Zola Tristar Grid Play Mat (5mm neoprene, rubber backing) gives the play surface a working layer for games with a lot of piece movement

What the warranty says about finish

Cosmetic damage — scratches, dents, and colour fading — isn't covered, which is standard for furniture finishes. A manufacturing defect in the finish is, and the two are genuinely different: a finish lifting or separating isn't wear, whereas a scratch is. Natural wood variation, including the Cerused Oak texture above, is explicitly not a defect. See Zola warranty coverage and Wear versus a defect.
