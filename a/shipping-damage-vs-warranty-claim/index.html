Shipping damage vs. warranty claim

Damage that happened in transit isn't a warranty claim — report it within 5 days of delivery with photos of the damage, the carton, and the shipping label, plus your order number, through our contact page at https://zolamod.com/pages/contact-us. It's the faster route for you anyway.

Short answer: damage that happened in transit is not a warranty claim — and the other route is faster for you.

The 5-day window on transit damage

Shipping damage has to be reported within 5 days of delivery. That deadline isn't ours — it's what the carrier's claims process requires, and it's why we can't do much about damage surfaced weeks later.

Two practical consequences:

  • Open and check everything promptly, even if you're not assembling for a while. A carton that looks fine outside can hold a cracked panel.
  • Keep the packaging until you've checked it all. Photos of the outer carton and shipping label are part of the claim, and once the boxes are flattened that evidence is gone.

Why transit damage goes the other way

The warranty covers manufacturing defects — an inadequacy in materials or workmanship that existed when you received the product. Damage from transport, loading, unloading, or handling is explicitly excluded, because it isn't a defect in the product.

That's not a dead end, though. It's a different, quicker desk. We don't need a claim form, a proof-of-purchase review, or a 3-5 business day evaluation window to send you a replacement part for a piece that got crushed on a truck. Photos and an order number are enough.

What to send

Send us your order number and clear photos of the damaged area through our contact page:

  1. Close-up shots of the damage
  2. The shipping label
  3. The boxes and packaging, if possible

The shipping label is the one people forget, and it's the piece that identifies the shipment when we take it up with the carrier.
