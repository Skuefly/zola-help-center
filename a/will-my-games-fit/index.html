Will my games fit?

Measure your biggest game's board plus player boards against the playable area — 36×36, 36×60, or 48×72 — rather than sizing by player count.

The honest way to size a table is to measure the biggest thing you actually play, rather than starting from how many people are at it.

The numbers to measure against

The playable area — the recessed vault — is what your game sits in:

TablePlayable area
Pangea 3×336″ × 36″ (91 × 91 cm)
Pangea 3×536″ × 60″ (91 × 152 cm)
Pangea 4×648″ × 72″ (122 × 183 cm)

Lay out your heaviest game on whatever you use now — main board, player boards, card rows, the tokens that spread — and measure the whole footprint. That number, not the box size, is what needs to fit.

Roughly what each size handles

  • 3×3 — most mid-weight boxes comfortably. Sized for dining nooks and compact rooms, seats 4.
  • 3×5 — heavier games with player boards spread out and space that doesn't feel tight. Seats 6.
  • 4×6 — the big sprawls: Twilight Imperium, Eclipse, large RPG maps, Warhammer. Seats 8–10, with room for each player's own area.

Two things people forget to include

Player boards spread outward. A game whose main board is 24″ square can occupy twice that once four players have their own areas laid out. That's usually what pushes people from a 3×3 to a 3×5.

The vault has depth. 2.5″ of it. Tall pieces, standing miniatures, and card holders sit in the recess rather than on a flat surface — so a game that feels cramped on a flat table often has more usable room here than the dimensions suggest.

The room is the other half

A table that fits your game still has to fit the room, with clearance for chairs. That's the constraint that decides it more often than the game does — see Will it fit my room?.

If you're between two sizes

Tell us the game, the group size, and the room, through our contact page. Sizing is easier to get right in a short conversation than from a table of numbers, and we'd rather talk you into the right one than sell you the larger one.

Worth knowing that Pangeas join — two 3×5s make a 3×10.5 or a 6.5×5 — so a second table later is a real option rather than a replacement. See Connecting two Pangea tables.
