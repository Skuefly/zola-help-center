Table dividers explained

Sold in 2-packs with an Omnitrack rail included, they split a surface into sections — 3FT $149.99, 4FT $179.99.

Dividers section a table's surface, so one large table can run as two areas — two games at once, a game and a working area, or a split layout for a bigger group.

DividerPrice
Pangea Table Divider 3FT (2 pack)$149.99
Pangea Table Divider 4FT (2 pack)$179.99

Both come in Dark Walnut and Cerused Oak, and each pack includes an Omnitrack rail. That's the important part — a divider isn't just a panel, it brings rail with it, so the new edge takes cup holders and other rail accessories the same way the perimeter does.

Choosing a length

The length needs to match the span it's crossing. As a rule the 3FT suits a 36″ span and the 4FT a 48″ one — but which is right depends on your table size and how the surface is currently configured, and that's not something to guess at.

Tell us your table size and what you're trying to do through our contact page and we'll confirm the right divider before you buy. Dividers tie into how the table is set up more than most accessories do, and it's a short conversation that avoids a return.

Where they're most useful

  • Running two games at once on a 4×6 or a joined configuration
  • Splitting a large surface so a big table doesn't feel cavernous for a small game
  • Changed layouts — a divider adds a rail edge where there wasn't one, which changes where accessories can go

Storage

They're solid pieces and need somewhere to live when they're out. The Storage Cube XL takes leaves and dividers as well as toppers, which the standard cube doesn't — see Where do the toppers go?.
